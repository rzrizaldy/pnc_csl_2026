Source: Family Table prototype, advisor view. Family data and figures are fictional; AI steps are simulated, formulas are real.

One workbench, four tools, one per stage. In Explore, the sale signal flags a likely sale twelve to eighteen months ahead from PNC’s own business-account data. The orange boxes on the screen show where each tool lives in the workbench. In Align, the family brief, shown on the left and boxed: four of five family members are now heard, up from one, the founder; their private check-ins become themes with no names; the widest divergence is fairness, at 1.00, scored by a fixed formula, and any gap above 0.75 brings in a Hawthorn facilitator. In Allocate, the meeting guide drafts a 90-minute agenda and the presentation screens. In Activate, the inbox drafts replies to the family and routes work to five specialist teams. Governance holds throughout: the AI prepares, and nothing reaches the family until the advisor approves it.
