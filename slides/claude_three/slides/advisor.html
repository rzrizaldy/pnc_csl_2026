Governance

AI analyses, scores, drafts and routes. The advisor approves every output before it reaches the client, and sees divergence, never individual responses.

Source: Family Table prototype. Scores, family data and figures are fictional; the signal model is a hypothesis to validate on PNC data.

The advisor workbench, stage by stage. In Explore, PNC’s own business-account data flags a likely sale a year before closing, and the advisor decides whether to engage. Then one AI tool for each core stage. Align: the family brief carries the core insight. Margaret believes her children agree, but the family’s widest divergence is on fairness, shown as a range with no names; Document AI also flags the 2012 will. Allocate: the AI turns the brief into a 90-minute agenda with presentation screens, and every figure comes from the calculator. Activate: the AI drafts client replies for the advisor to approve, routes work to five specialist teams, and flags the $8 to 9 million heading to heirs who bank elsewhere. The governance rule holds throughout: the advisor approves every output before any client action. This addresses Alex’s friction: hearing only the founder, undocumented concerns, and capabilities spread across business units.
