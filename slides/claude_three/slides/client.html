What the family gets

Clarity: their own number, not a guess. Fairness: a private voice, without a fight at dinner. Confidence: every answer is checked by a person before it reaches them.

Source: Family Table prototype (tablet view). The Wilson family, portraits and figures are fictional; AI steps are simulated, formulas are real.

Five moments on the family side, all on a phone. Explore and Onboard are the lead-in; the core journey, where the family actually decides, is Align, Allocate and Activate. Before Margaret ever talks to PNC, the preview shows what a sale could mean for a family like hers. Once she joins, PNC has already filled in the facts; she ranks her goals and says what fair means to her. Claire checks in privately: natural-language processing tags themes, and she decides what Alex may read. Each child sees their own number under every split, votes privately, and a decision tree narrows 12 possible plans to one. Questions go to Alex, not a chatbot: the AI drafts, Alex checks and sends. This answers Margaret’s friction: a guided portal, not a dense dashboard, and no direct confrontation at the dinner table.
