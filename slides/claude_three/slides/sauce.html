Core innovation

A consented family-intelligence layer on PNC’s own commercial signal. AI reads and explains, formulas do the money, and the advisor decides, seeing gaps but never anyone’s words, and only when 3 or more take part.

Wilson figures are fictional; the fee is an illustrative 0.8% blended rate, to validate with PNC. Signal use needs PNC compliance review.

Generative AI and alerts are easy to copy; these three assets are not. First, timing: PNC banks the operating company, so its own account data can flag a sale a year before closing, while outside wealth managers see the money only after it moves. Second, private-by-default family intelligence: natural-language processing turns each member’s private words into themes inside their own vault, a fixed formula scores the gaps, and Alex sees the size of a disagreement, never the words, and only when at least three people take part. Honest answers given in private are data no competitor can buy or scrape. Third, one household graph links the business to every heir, so the next generation meets PNC before the money moves. The added value: for the family, the fairness question surfaces before the letter of intent. For PNC, in the Wilson case, $27.0M stays, relationships grow from three to five, $8.5M reaches the next generation at PNC, and the fees are about $216K a year at an illustrative 0.8%.
