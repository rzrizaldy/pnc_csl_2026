One sale. Two sides. Four decisions.

1 · Invite

Walt requests help with his sale. Alex confirms engagement scope and conflicts. Each family member can accept, decline or ask for a separate conversation.

2 · Choose what to share

Sloane reviews her answers. Nothing goes to Alex until she chooses to share. Even unnamed themes can identify someone in a small family.

3 · Prepare, then decide

Alex reviews shared concerns and routes document questions to counsel. The family compares scenarios and records next steps. Account introductions are optional.

A concrete AI job, with a human checkpoint

Proposed AI

Extract candidate themes from a member’s words; retrieve document passages; draft an advisor brief from authorized sources. Member confirms themes; advisor reviews brief; counsel decides legal meaning.

Working demo

Local keyword tagging, role-based navigation and deterministic proceeds and gift calculations. Assistant answers and document findings are scripted. No bank systems, live models or secure vault are connected.

Pilot evaluation

Use a permissioned, expert-labeled test set. Measure unsupported statements, extraction accuracy and privacy failures. Any unauthorized disclosure stops the pilot. Model selection follows evaluation, not branding.

The business case is a testable hypothesis

Hale example: $27M

48 − 8 contingent earnout − 3.5 debt − 1.5 fees − 8 estimated tax = $27M illustrative closing cash. Proposed buckets: $9M reserve, $12M gifts, $6M long-term. This is available cash, not retained assets.

Heirs: $9–10.0M

Dale, Ross and Sloane’s combined gift varies with the split. It is part of the $27M, never additional AUM. Kyle and Ross’s $2M buyer equity is assumed separate from Walt’s offer; confirm actual capitalization and gift tax.

Illustrative funding ask

Cap a 90-day, 10-family feasibility pilot at $150k: $90k implementation, $40k review and testing, $20k operations. Planning assumptions, not a PNC estimate. At an assumed 0.50% annual gross fee, $30M incremental fee-bearing AUM covers $150k before costs; profitability needs a separate model.

Why PNC could win

An existing relationship

A commercial relationship may support an earlier, owner-authorized introduction. Competitors can also bank the business. Do not assume exclusive data or permission to reuse transactions.

An accountable handoff

The proposed advantage is coordination across relationship managers, wealth advisors and family specialists, with recorded permissions and review ownership.

Earned over time

Test whether family-approved history and reliable service improve continuity. The interface and models are copyable; outcome evidence and operational adoption would have to build the advantage.

What we know, and what we must learn

No primary interview evidence is in this repository. The Hales and their quotes are fictional; Succession is an optional design analogy, not customer discovery. Read the inspiration and its limits.

PNC’s Begin at the end discusses preparing for life after a sale. It supports the problem framing, not our proposed workflow or retention claim.

Trust · first 30 days

Interview two owners/heirs and two advisors separately. Ask who should see each answer, test sharing comprehension and offer a non-digital route. Record actual quotes and permission to cite them.

Feasibility · 90 days

Ten opt-in families, five advisors. Target ≥80% correct sharing-comprehension responses and ≥25% lower median preparation time against each advisor’s baseline. Completion is not proof of honesty; collect private exit interviews.

Business outcome · 12 months

Track voluntary introductions and incremental fee-bearing assets against a matched comparison group, net of internal transfers. Report denominators and attrition. Ten families cannot establish causal retention lift.
