Margaret is selling the business she built. Her family has never talked about what's fair.

Family Table gives the family one private, guided place to plan the sale together. It gives their PNC advisor the whole family's view a year before closing, instead of the founder's alone.

How one sale moves between them

Six stages, two lanes. At each stage something one side does changes what the other side sees. The demo walks it in ten steps, alternating sides, from a preview before PNC to one year after the sale.

What the AI does, and doesn't

AI reads, sorts and explains. Fixed formulas do the money. People make every decision. In the prototype the AI is simulated; in production, Claude or GPT sit behind one PNC gateway with the same guardrails.

Why only PNC can build this

A signal PNC already holds

PNC banks the business, so it can see a sale coming a year before closing. Outside wealth managers see the money only after it moves.

Private by default

Each person answers alone. The advisor sees gaps, not words. Honest answers are data no competitor can buy or scrape.

One household graph

Business, owners, spouse, heirs, trusts and accounts in one consented map, with Hawthorn IFS facilitators.

What PNC gets, and what we still need to prove

Value for PNC (Wilson example)

$27.0M lands at closing: the balance PNC keeps only if the family chooses to stay.
2 of 3 heirs bank elsewhere today; Family Table meets them before $8–9M reaches them.
One advisor hears the whole family before the letter of intent.

Hypotheses to test in a pilot

1. Family members answer honestly when answers stay private. Measure: check-in completion above 70%.
2. Advisors act earlier on the signal. Measure: first family meeting before the letter of intent.
3. Heirs open a PNC account. Measure: next-gen accounts per family within 12 months.
